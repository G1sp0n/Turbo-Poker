    CheckPass without betting. The turn moves on to the next player.Only if nobody has bet
    BetPut chips in first. Everyone after you has to call, raise or fold.Only if nobody has bet · at least the big blind
    CallMatch the current bet to stay in the hand.After someone bets
    RaiseMake the bet bigger. Everyone has to respond to the new amount.After someone bets · see min raise
    FoldGive up your cards and anything you've already put in this hand.Any time it's your turn
    All-inPush every chip you have. You can't be forced out of the hand after that.Any time it's your turn

    A betting round ends when everyone still in has put in the same amount, or everyone has checked. If all but one player fold, the last player wins the pot right away and doesn't have to show their cards.

      04All the rules

      Settle any argument here
      Setting up
      1. Pick the first dealer. Everyone draws one card. Highest card gets the dealer button. Aces are high.
      2. Starting chips. Everyone starts with the same stack, shown on the game page under Chips. The rest stays in the bank for change.
      3. No rebuys. Once you lose all your chips, you're out. Nobody buys back in.
      4. Seats. Keep the same seats all game. The button moving around makes it fair.
      Every hand
      1. Blinds. The player to the left of the dealer button posts the small blind. The next player to the left posts the big blind. Use the amounts on the timer.
      2. Dealing. Deal one card at a time, starting with the player left of the button and going round twice, so everyone gets two cards face down.
      3. Order of a hand. Bet, burn one card and deal the flop (3 cards), bet, burn one and deal the turn (1 card), bet, burn one and deal the river (1 card), bet, then showdown.
      4. Who acts first. Before the flop: the player left of the big blind. On the flop, turn and river: the first player still in to the left of the button.
      5. Play goes left. The turn always passes to the left, clockwise around the table.
      6. Moving the button. After every hand the dealer button moves one seat to the left. The blinds move with it.
      7. Heads-up (2 players). The dealer posts the small blind and acts first before the flop, then last on every later round.
      8. Short blind. If a player can't cover their blind, they put in what they have and are all-in.
      Betting
      1. Minimum bet. The smallest bet is the big blind.
      2. Minimum raise. A raise must be at least as big as the last bet or raise. Blinds 25/50, someone raises to 150 (+100), so the next raise is to at least 250.
      3. No limit. You can bet or raise up to everything in front of you at any time.
      4. Say it or do it in one go. Say "raise" before you put chips in, or push the whole amount in one move. Going back to the stack for more chips (a string bet) only counts as a call.
      5. One chip, no words. Putting in a single bigger chip without saying anything counts as a call.
      6. Wait your turn. Acting out of turn isn't allowed. If you do it, the action stands only if nothing changes before your real turn.
      7. Short all-in. An all-in smaller than a full raise doesn't reopen betting for players who already acted. They can only call or fold.
      8. Chips stay in sight. Keep your chips on the table where everyone can see them. Higher chips in front, so people can count your stack.
      All-ins and side pots
      1. All-in limit. A player can only win from each opponent as much as they put in. Everything above that goes to a side pot they can't win.
      2. Side pots. Use the Side pots calculator on the game page. Pay the last side pot first, then work back to the main pot.
      3. Show when all-in. Once someone is all-in and no more betting can happen, everyone still in turns their cards face up before the rest of the board is dealt.
      Showdown
      1. Best five cards. Make your best five-card hand from your two cards plus the five on the table. You can use both, one, or none of yours.
      2. Who shows first. The last player to bet or raise on the river shows first. If everyone checked, the first player left of the button shows first.
      3. Cards speak. The best hand wins, even if the player called it wrong. Turn all cards face up and let the table read them.
      4. Suits don't count. Suits never break a tie. Two equal hands split the pot.
      5. Kickers. If two players have the same pair or set, the highest other card (the kicker) decides it. If all five cards match, split the pot.
      6. Odd chip. When a split pot doesn't divide evenly, the extra chip goes to the winner closest to the left of the button.
      7. Mucking. If you lose, you can throw your cards away face down without showing them.
      Mistakes
      1. Misdeal. If the first or second card comes out face up, or someone gets the wrong number of cards, collect everything, reshuffle and deal again. Same button, same blinds.
      2. Card flipped later. If a card is shown by accident after that, the player keeps it and the hand goes on.
      3. Too early on the board. If the dealer turns a board card before the betting is finished, that card is put aside and replaced with the next one after the betting.
      4. Wrong chips in. If someone puts in the wrong amount by mistake, fix it before the next player acts.
      Turbo Poker rules
      1. Levels. The blinds go up when the timer runs out. A hand that has started is played out at the old blinds.
      2. Low League. The first levels use white 1s, red 5s and blue 10s.
      3. Switching to Big League. At 3 players left (2 players left in a 3 or 4 player game), or after Level 5 at the latest (after Level 4 heads-up), everyone finishes the hand and swaps up.
      4. Colouring up. Count your stack, multiply it by the number chosen on the game page, round up to the next 25 and swap for green 25s and black 100s.
      5. Knocked out. When you lose every chip, you're out. Press Out on the game board so it knows how many players are left.
      6. Winning. The game ends when one player has every chip. That player wins.
